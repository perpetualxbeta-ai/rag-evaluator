# Halcyon Robotics: Company Overview

> **Note:** Halcyon Robotics and all of its products are fictional. This corpus exists only to exercise the rag-evaluator pipeline.

## Who we are

Halcyon Robotics designs and builds autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) for warehouses and distribution centres. The company was founded in 2019 in Rotterdam, the Netherlands, by Ingrid Vos (CEO) and Tomasz Kowalczyk (CTO), who previously worked together on automated container handling at the port of Rotterdam. Their founding idea was that small and mid-sized warehouses needed robots that could be deployed in days rather than months, without installing floor markers, magnetic tape or other fixed infrastructure.

Halcyon's headquarters and main engineering centre remain in Rotterdam. The company opened a North American sales and support office in Austin, Texas, in 2022, and an Asia-Pacific office in Singapore in 2024. As of mid-2026 Halcyon employs about 340 people, roughly half of them in engineering.

## Products

Halcyon sells three things that are designed to work together:

- **Kestrel robots.** The Kestrel K1, launched in 2021, carried payloads of up to 150 kg. It was succeeded in March 2025 by the **Kestrel K2**, which doubled the payload to 300 kg and introduced opportunity charging. The K1 is no longer sold, but existing K1 units continue to receive security updates until the end of 2027.
- **HalcyonOS.** The firmware that runs on every Kestrel robot. It handles navigation, obstacle avoidance and safety functions, and it is updated over the air. The K2 shipped with HalcyonOS 3.0.
- **Halcyon Fleet.** A web-based fleet manager that assigns tasks, plans routes for many robots at once, and shows battery, health and utilisation data for the whole fleet. Halcyon Fleet is sold as an annual subscription priced per robot.

Robots are charged on **DockPoint** chargers, which are wall-mounted stations that the robot drives onto by itself.

## Customers and deployments

Halcyon's customers are mostly third-party logistics providers, e-commerce fulfilment centres and spare-parts distributors. By mid-2026 more than 210 customer sites in 14 countries were running Kestrel robots, with a combined fleet of around 4,800 units. The largest single deployment, at a grocery distribution centre near Antwerp, runs 160 Kestrel K2 robots across two floors, using the elevator integration introduced in HalcyonOS 3.1.

A typical new deployment goes from site survey to first robots in production in about three weeks. During the site survey, Halcyon engineers map the facility, mark "shared zones" where robots and people work side by side, and agree on the speed limits that apply in those zones.

## Approach to safety and support

Halcyon treats safety as a product feature rather than an add-on. Every robot ships with four emergency stop buttons and certified safety-rated laser scanners, and every operator must complete the Kestrel Operator Certification before working alongside the robots. The details are in the Safety Procedures document.

Customers can choose between Standard and Premium support plans. The plans, together with the warranty terms for robots and batteries, are described in the Warranty and Support document.
